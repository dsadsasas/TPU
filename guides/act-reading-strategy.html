Build a quick passage map

Notice who or what the passage is about, how each paragraph contributes, and where the author changes direction. A mental label such as problem, example, or objection helps you relocate information without memorizing every detail.

Distinguish detail from interpretation

For a direct detail, locate the relevant sentence and paraphrase it accurately. For an inference, combine only what the passage supports. For tone, look at the author’s word choices rather than your own reaction to the topic.

Handle broad questions after understanding structure

A main-idea answer should cover the passage’s central focus without exaggerating it. A title-like phrase may be too vague. A highly specific answer may describe just one paragraph. Check both scope and accuracy.

Keep a purposeful pace

Practice with official materials matching your administration’s format. When two choices remain, identify the precise difference and look for evidence that settles it. If the evidence is not clear and time is limited, choose your best answer and move forward.

LET’S WORK THROUGH IT

Original worked example

Original passage: “Nora expected the archive to be silent. Instead, volunteers debated dates, compared letters, and asked her to join them. By closing time, she had signed up for another shift.” How does Nora’s experience change?

She moves from expecting a quiet setting to participating in an active, collaborative one. Signing up again supports interest in returning; it does not establish that she has chosen a career.

A tip worth keeping

Leave a short mental label for each paragraph. This saves time when a question sends you back to a detail.

Your quick checklist

  • Identify the exact task before working.
  • Show the reasoning, not just the answer.
  • Review the mistake and try a new example.
PUT IT INTO PRACTICE

Try it yourself

These original practice questions were written for this guide. Try each one before opening its explanation.

1. What detail supports Nora’s interest in returning?

Show answer & explanation

She signed up for another shift.

2. Can we infer that Nora dislikes quiet places?

Show answer & explanation

No. Her expectation does not establish a general preference.

3. Which better describes the archive: collaborative or deserted?

Show answer & explanation

Collaborative. Volunteers discuss information and invite Nora to participate.
